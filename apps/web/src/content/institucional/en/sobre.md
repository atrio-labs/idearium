## What it is

Idearium is the public space where ideas with potential for social impact become structured proposals (RFCs) and attract volunteers willing to build them together, in the open.

<!-- fonte: VISAO §2 -->
## Mission

To end the silent death of good ideas.

Some people have a repertoire of problems: they see the problem every day, they know who suffers from it, they sense the solution, but they don't build. Others have a repertoire of solutions: they can program, design, write, draw, but have no idea where to apply it. Idearium is the infrastructure that connects those two repertoires without going through a commercial gatekeeper, an investment fund or a social network.

<!-- fonte: VISAO §3 -->
## Vision

In five years we want to be the Lusophone reference in open civic tech: the place where foundations, universities, collectives and volunteers prototype solutions to local problems. In Portuguese and in English. With thousands of published RFCs, hundreds turned into real code, and dozens turned into lasting organizations. Sustained by individual supporters and institutional grants, never by fees on the people who use it.

Idearium does not become a startup. It becomes an institution.

## Values

- **Radical openness.** All content is published under CC BY-SA 4.0. No RFC has an owner once published; it has an original author, with permanent credit.
- **Shared credit.** Whoever proposes, whoever comments constructively and whoever builds all appear in the credits of the final implementation. There is no hierarchy of merit.
- **Respected pseudonym.** Real identity is always optional. The `@username` is the only thing we ask for.
- **No financial mediation.** The platform never touches money in transactions between people. No fees, no revenue share, no brokered bounties.
- **Volunteering as the base.** People build here because they want to. Whoever wants to be paid does it outside the platform, and that is fine.
- **Support-funded.** The model is Wikimedia's: individual donors and institutional grants. Never paying users.
- **Editorial aesthetics.** We are closer to Wikipedia, the IETF and arXiv than to Product Hunt.
- **Public decisions.** Governance is transparent, and important changes are RFCs too.

## How we think about Idearium

**Labs.** A lab is a thematic area that organizes RFCs, such as Education or Cities. It is not a silo: an RFC belongs to one lab, but anyone moves freely between all of them.

**RFCs.** An RFC (Request For Comments) is an idea put on paper in a way that other people can comment on, improve and build. When published, it gets a permanent number (`RFC-0007`), an author, a lab, a language and a public version history. RFCs can be edited, and nothing is lost.

**Behaviors, not roles.** There are no "creators" and "builders" as kinds of people. There are three things a person does, at different times:

- **propose**: write an RFC;
- **discuss**: comment, refine, challenge;
- **build**: enroll in an RFC and contribute code, design or text.

Enrolling is light. It needs no approval, it is not a promise, and anyone who enrolls can leave at any time. Leaving is public.

## The path of an RFC

1. **Draft.** Only the author sees it.
2. **In discussion.** The RFC is published, has a number, and is open to comments and enrollments.
3. **In building.** At least one person has enrolled and people are working, with progress updates.
4. **Published.** The implementation is out and the idea became a product or project.

There is also an **archived** state, for RFCs with no activity. It is reversible. Nothing is deleted. States describe the current temperature of the idea, not its worth.

## What we are and what we are not

We are:

- an editorial-civic platform of community RFCs;
- a space for volunteers to meet, between those who propose and those who build;
- an open repository with a free license (CC BY-SA 4.0);
- bilingual (pt-BR and en) from launch;
- a digital commons, not a product.

We are not:

- a showcase of launched products, like Product Hunt;
- a place focused on profit and recurring revenue, like IndieHackers;
- a freelancer marketplace;
- a social network with an algorithmic feed;
- an incubator or accelerator;
- a platform with points, badges, leaderboards or streaks;
- a service that charges its users or has a premium tier;
- a place with ads;
- an intermediary of financial transactions;
- something that aims to grow to millions of daily users.

The project's own vision text is the reference: what it says prevails over any loose idea. To see how that text changes, read the [governance](/en/governanca) page.
